The thyroid is a small, butterfly-shaped gland located in the neck. It produces hormones that influence metabolism, energy, body temperature, digestion and heart rate. When the thyroid produces too little or too much hormone, it can affect your weight, energy levels, mood and overall well-being.

## Understanding Thyroid Disorders

The two most common thyroid disorders are hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism.

### Hypothyroidism

Hypothyroidism, also known as an underactive thyroid, occurs when the thyroid gland does not produce enough hormones. Common symptoms may include:

* Fatigue and weakness
* Unexplained weight gain
* Sensitivity to cold
* Constipation
* Dry skin or thinning hair
* Difficulty concentrating
* Low mood
* Joint or muscle discomfort
* Heavy or irregular periods

These symptoms may develop slowly and can also occur with other health conditions. Therefore, thyroid problems should be diagnosed by a doctor through appropriate blood tests.

### Hyperthyroidism

Hyperthyroidism occurs when the thyroid produces excessive hormones. It may cause unintentional weight loss, anxiety, heat sensitivity, increased sweating, trembling, disturbed sleep and a rapid heartbeat.

Because the nutritional needs of hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism may differ, avoid following a generic thyroid diet without understanding your condition.

## Can Thyroid Problems Cause Weight Gain?

An underactive thyroid may slow metabolism and contribute to weight gain. However, not all weight gain is caused by thyroid dysfunction. Some thyroid-related weight increase can also be associated with salt and water retention.

## Important Nutrients for Thyroid Health

### Iodine

The thyroid uses iodine to produce hormones. Sources include iodised salt, dairy products, eggs and seafood.

However, excessive iodine does not improve thyroid function and may worsen certain thyroid disorders. Avoid kelp, seaweed or high-dose iodine supplements unless recommended by your doctor.

### Selenium

Selenium supports processes involved in thyroid hormone metabolism. It can be obtained from eggs, fish, chicken, whole grains, sunflower seeds and nuts. Supplements should only be taken under professional guidance, as excessive selenium may be harmful.

### Zinc

Zinc contributes to metabolism, immune function and hormone-related processes. Good sources include lentils, beans, dairy products, eggs, pumpkin seeds, whole grains, meat and poultry.

### Protein

Protein supports muscle health, fullness and weight management. Include foods such as dal, beans, paneer, curd, tofu, eggs, chicken or fish in your meals.

A fitness nutritionist Mohali can help adjust your protein intake according to your health, activity level and fitness goals.

### Fibre

People with hypothyroidism may experience constipation. Vegetables, fruit, oats, whole grains, lentils, beans, chia seeds and flaxseeds provide dietary fibre.

Increase fibre gradually and drink sufficient water. Discuss meal and supplement timing with your doctor because certain foods and supplements may affect thyroid medication absorption.

## What Should You Eat for Better Thyroid Health?

A balanced thyroid-friendly meal may include:

* Half a plate of vegetables or salad
* One-quarter plate of protein-rich food
* One-quarter plate of roti, rice or another whole grain
* A small portion of healthy fat
* Water or an unsweetened drink

## Thyroid Medicine and Meal Timing

Hypothyroidism is commonly treated with levothyroxine. Food, calcium, iron and certain supplements may affect its absorption. Levothyroxine is often taken consistently on an empty stomach, but always follow your doctor’s instructions.

Do not stop or change your medicine based on dietary advice. Tell your nutrition consultant Mohali about your medicines and supplements so that your meal schedule can be planned appropriately.
